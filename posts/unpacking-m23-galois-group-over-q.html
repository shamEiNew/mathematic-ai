Unpacking “$M_{23}$ is a Galois group over $\mathbb{Q}$” — Verification Roadmap

August 13, 2026 • Sham Group Theory Galois Theory Sporadic Groups Open Source

Repo: github.com/shamEiNew/mathieu-group • Live project site: shameinew.github.io/mathieu-group

Paper: Huang, Jackson, Lee, Poonen, Pries, Zhang (Aug 2026), arxiv/M23.tex. Paper link: arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08538. Authors' code (Magma): github.com/shaowuz/m23isgalois.

The paper closes a 40-year-old gap: 25 of the 26 sporadic simple groups were realized as Galois groups over $\mathbb{Q}$ during 1984–1989; $M_{23}$ was the last holdout. This repo unpacks every step, attempts to verify everything with open-source tools, and tries to explain the result at different levels of background.

Summary of $M_{23}$

The $M_{23}$ is a simple group of order 10,200,960 and it's part of the 26 exceptions (sporadic groups) per the classification of finite simple groups. $M_{23}$ is one of the five groups introduced by Émile Léonard Mathieu. It's a multiply transitive permutation group on 23 objects.

Open-source re-verification matters

The paper's final certification uses Magma (closed source). The single most valuable technical contribution would be a crowd effort that makes an independent verification chain using only software like GAP, PARI, Python, etc. Moreover, it helps us assimilate the construction in a much better way, as the authors themselves suggest several questions that are left unanswered.

3 $M_{23}$ has 17 conjugacy classes; $G_\mathbb{Q}$ fixes 2A and swaps 23A↔23B via $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$ ✅ classes done GAP (23A/23B verified mutually inverse; Galois-action writeup pending)
4 No $G_\mathbb{Q}$-stable class triple is rigid ($|N_i| \ne 1$ always) — the classical method provably fails todo GAP: structure constants from character table, inclusion–exclusion over subgroups
5 For (2A, 23A, 23B): $|N_i| = 7$ — exactly seven covers ✅ done GAP — Frobenius formula gives $|\bar\Sigma|/|G| = 7$; generation forced (only $23{:}11$ has order divisible by 23, and it is involution-free)
6 Genus of $X$ is 4 (Riemann–Hurwitz) todo hand + Sage double-check
7 Numerical Belyi map: power series of the four 1-forms, canonical model $P=Q=0$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$ hard replicate Hejhal/KMSV algorithm in Python/Julia (authors used Magma BelyiDB)
8 Coefficient recognition: $P, Q$ have coefficients in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$ (the “miracle”) todo PSLQ/LLL via mpmath or fpylll, starting from published $P, Q$
9 Riemann–Roch constructions: $u$ with $\mathrm{div}(u)=23b'-23c'$, $y \in L(5b'-c')$, $v = y \cdot y'$ todo SageMath / Singular function-field machinery
10 $F(T,V) \in \mathbb{Z}[T,V]$, $\deg_T = 8$, $\deg_V = 23$; disc factors as $c \cdot t^8(t^2+23)^{88}h_{84}^2$ todo PARI/GP or Sage (pull $F$ from authors' repo)
11 $G_{\mathrm{geom}} = M_{23}$ via reduction mod 31 + tame specialization todo GAP+PARI replication of the Magma certificate; the SGA1 specialization step is the key mathematical input to explain
12 The two explicit degree-23 polynomials have Galois group $M_{23}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, ramified only in $\{2,3,23\}$ / $\{2,7,23\}$ ◖ partial PARI — code/pari/dedekind.gp: irreducible, disc square ($\Rightarrow \subseteq A_{23}$), nonsolvable, 299/299 prime types are $M_{23}$ types $\Rightarrow$ Gal $\in \{M_{23}, A_{23}\}$. Remaining: Steiner resolvent for $M_{23}$ vs $A_{23}$ (see below)

Claim 12 is an open-tooling gap

PARI's polgalois stops at degree 11. A rigorous open certification that $\mathrm{Gal}(f) = M_{23}$:

  • Lower bound (contains $M_{23}$): $f$ irreducible $\Rightarrow$ transitive of prime degree 23 $\Rightarrow$ contains a 23-cycle; factorization patterns mod $p$ (Dedekind) rule out the solvable transitive groups of degree 23; only $M_{23}$, $A_{23}$, $S_{23}$ remain.
  • Upper bound (inside $M_{23}$): $\mathrm{disc}(f)$ square $\Rightarrow$ inside $A_{23}$. Separating $M_{23}$ from $A_{23}$ is the hard part — cycle types cannot do it ($M_{23}$'s types are a subset of $A_{23}$'s). Route: resolvent for the Steiner system $S(4,7,23)$ — exhibit a rational root of the degree-253 “heptad-stabilizer” resolvent, computed with rigorous precision bounds (LLL / p-adic lifting). Ideal Rust project (exact arithmetic + speed).

Explanation ladder

  1. Level 0 — no math: symmetry as shuffling; the 26 “sporadic snowflakes”; a 40-year treasure hunt with one last missing piece; what it means that a plain whole-number equation has exactly this rare symmetry. One picture: 7 dots, Galois conjugation swirls them around, and one dot doesn't move — the miracle.
  2. Level 1 — curious reader / journalist: what a Galois group measures, why “over $\mathbb{Q}$” is the whole difficulty, why computers + a miracle + classical theory each carried a third of the load. FAQ format.
  3. Level 2 — undergrad: Jupyter Book chapters — permutation groups, $M_{23}$ and the Steiner system, covers of $\mathbb{P}^1$ and monodromy, Riemann existence, rigidity, why rigidity fails here, Belyi maps and dessins d'enfants, descent and fields of moduli. Every chapter ends in runnable code.
  4. Level 3 — expert: full reworked proof with no “we omit the proof” left standing (e.g. the $h_{84}^2$ singularity claim the authors explicitly skip).

Formalization (scope)

Full formalization of the theorem is out of reach short-term (the numerical Belyi steps don't need formalizing anyway — only the algebraic certificate does).

Done: formal/M23Formal — a dependency-free Lean 4 package, builds green: the triple is a set of genuine permutations, $g_1 \circ g_2 \circ g_3 = \mathrm{id}$, orders 2/23/23, cycle-type witnesses — all kernel-checked by decide. formal/MathlibSkeleton/M23.lean states the deeper targets ($M_{23} \le A_{23}$, $|M_{23}| = 10{,}200{,}960$, 4-transitivity, simplicity) with proof plans.

Realistic Lean 4 milestones, in order:

  1. $M_{23}$ as a permutation group: generators, order, simplicity (Mathlib has none of the Mathieu groups — a valuable standalone contribution). Key infrastructure: a formalized stabilizer-chain (Schreier–Sims) certificate checker.
  2. Dedekind's theorem (factorization mod $p$ $\Rightarrow$ cycle type in the Galois group) — partially in Mathlib.
  3. The classification of transitive groups of prime degree 23 (7 groups).
  4. The resolvent certificate for $\mathrm{Gal}(f) \subseteq M_{23}$, verified by exact arithmetic.
